# formatDate and parseDate

`formatDate(date, { locale })` writes a date in the locale's numbers, such as `10/13/2026` in `en-US`, `13.10.2026` in `de` and `2026. 10. 13.` in `ko`. `parseDate(text, { locale })` reads typed text back into a `Temporal.PlainDate`, or returns `null` when the text is not a date. Without `locale`, both use the runtime's own. [Field.DateInput](/field/date-input) reads and writes its text with them, and they work in a [Field.TextInput](/field/text-input) of your own too.

## What parseDate reads {/*#reading-dates*/}

It reads a date in numbers in the order `formatDate` writes them or in the order of the locale's `short` date style, as `13.10.26` in `gsw`, whose `formatDate` writes `2026-10-13`. When both orders read a date, a year of four or more digits is read in `formatDate`'s order, and a year of one or two digits in the `short` style's order when that style writes the year in two digits. It also reads a date as `Intl.DateTimeFormat` writes it in the locale's `medium`, `long` or `full` date style, such as `Oct 13, 2026` or `Tuesday, October 13, 2026` in `en-US`.

Letter case doesn't matter, digits may be in any script, and the parts may be separated by any punctuation, symbols or spaces. The month may be any of its long or short names, such as `Oct` or `October`. A weekday may be long, short or left out, but must be the date's. The style's other words, such as `de` in `13 de octubre de 2026`, may be replaced by a space. Three numbers that start with a year of four or more digits, such as `2026-10-13`, are read as year, month and day, unless the locale writes the year first in another order. A year is a Gregorian year, whatever the locale's own calendar. A year of one or two digits is read as the nearest such year to the current one, so a date about 50 years away needs all four digits, and a year of three digits is not read.

## Temporal

Todae ships no Temporal polyfill. Both use `Temporal.PlainDate`, so where the browser has no Temporal, the app loads a polyfill (see [Native platform](/platform)).

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** Text in the `locale` you pass, or the runtime's own when you pass none.

**You supply:** A `locale` that matches the page's, and the same one for writing and reading. A message near the input when `parseDate` returns `null`.

**Watch for:** On a server with no `locale`, the text uses the server's locale. Text that reads as a date but not the one meant, such as a two-digit year read as the nearest such year: show the date in words nearby when that matters.

## When to use

### formatDate

**When:** Writing a date as text for a locale, such as into a text input of your own.

**When not:** A date with a weekday or a month name → `Intl.DateTimeFormat`.

### parseDate

**When:** Reading a date someone typed.

**When not:** Text in a fixed format such as `2026-10-13` → `Temporal.PlainDate.from`.
