# formatDateTime and parseDateTime

`formatDateTime(value, { locale })` writes the date in numbers, as [formatDate](/utilities/format-date) does, and the time in hours and minutes, or with seconds when it has seconds, such as `10/13/2026, 9:30 PM` in `en-US`, `13.10.2026, 21:30` in `de` and `2026. 10. 13. 오후 9:30` in `ko`. `parseDateTime(text, { locale })` reads typed text back into a `Temporal.PlainDateTime`, or returns `null` when the text is not one. Without `locale`, both use the runtime's own. [Field.DateTimeInput](/field/date-time-input) reads and writes its text with them.

## What parseDateTime reads {/*#reading-date-times*/}

It reads a date as [`parseDate`](/utilities/format-date#reading-dates) does, with its year in full or as its last two digits, then a time as [`parseTime`](/utilities/format-time#reading-times) does, in the order the locale writes them, with or without the words the locale writes between them, such as `Oct 13, 2026 at 9:30 PM` or `10/13/2026 9:30 pm` in `en-US`. The time needs its minutes, as in `21:30` or `2130`, or a word, as in `9 pm`, so an hour alone, as in `13.10.2026 21`, is not read, and neither is a date without a time. Text that also reads as a month and day without a year and a time, as `10/13 12 05 pm` in `en-US`, is not read.

## Temporal

Todae ships no Temporal polyfill. Both use `Temporal.PlainDateTime`, so where the browser has no Temporal, the app loads a polyfill (see [Native platform](/platform)). A `PlainDateTime` has no time zone: it is the date and time on the user's clock. To store an instant, combine it with a time zone in your own code, such as `value.toZonedDateTime(zone)`.

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** Text in the `locale` you pass, or the runtime's own when you pass none.

**You supply:** A `locale` that matches the page's, and the same one for writing and reading. A message near the input when `parseDateTime` returns `null`.

**Watch for:** On a server with no `locale`, the text uses the server's locale. A value stored as if it were an instant: a `PlainDateTime` moves with the reader's clock, so add the time zone before you store a moment.

## When to use

### formatDateTime

**When:** Writing a date and time as text for a locale, such as into a text input of your own.

**When not:** A date and time with a weekday or a month name → `Intl.DateTimeFormat`. How long ago a time was → [formatRelativeTime](/utilities/format-relative-time).

### parseDateTime

**When:** Reading a date and time someone typed.

**When not:** Text in a fixed format such as `2026-10-13T09:30` → `Temporal.PlainDateTime.from`.
