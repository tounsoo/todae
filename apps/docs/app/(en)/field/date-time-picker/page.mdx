# Field.DateTimePicker

`Field.DateTimePicker` is a button inside a [Field](/field) that opens a [Calendar](/calendar) and a list of times in a [Popover](/popover), side by side, or the list under the calendar on a narrow screen. Its `value` is a `Temporal.PlainDateTime` or `null`. `onValueChange` gets the date and time each time a pick leaves a date the calendar offers and a time in range for it. Picking a time while the calendar holds a date it offers closes the popover. It works like [Field.DatePicker](/field/date-picker) and [Field.TimePicker](/field/time-picker), and takes the same props where they mean the same thing. A `PlainDateTime` has no time zone: it is the date and time on the user's clock. To store an instant, combine it with a time zone in your own code, such as `value.toZonedDateTime(zone)`.

```tsx
const id = useId();
const [start, setStart] = useState<Temporal.PlainDateTime | null>(null);
const [text, setText] = useState('');
const [invalid, setInvalid] = useState(false);

<Field.Group>
  <Label.Root htmlFor={id}>Meeting starts</Label.Root>
  <Field.Root id={id} invalid={invalid}>
    <Field.TextInput
      value={text}
      onChange={(event) => {
        setText(event.target.value);
        setStart(parseDateTime(event.target.value));
        setInvalid(false);
      }}
      onBlur={() => {
        if (start) setText(formatDateTime(start));
        else setInvalid(text.trim() !== '');
      }}
    />
    <Field.DateTimePicker
      value={start}
      onValueChange={(next) => {
        setStart(next);
        setText(formatDateTime(next));
        setInvalid(false);
      }}
      step={15}
      showValue={false}
    />
  </Field.Root>
</Field.Group>
```

Here a [Field.TextInput](/field/text-input) in the same field lets people type the date and time too. A picked date and time is written into the text with `formatDateTime`. Beside a `Field.TextInput`, that input is the field's control. It takes the field's `id`, `name` and description, and a press elsewhere in the field focuses it. With `showValue={false}`, the button shows only a calendar glyph. The button sends the date and time with a form under its own `name` if it has one, and otherwise under the field's `name`, unless a Field part or a control joined with [`useField`](/utilities/use-field), other than a `Field.ColorPicker`, `Field.DatePicker`, `Field.TimePicker` or another `Field.DateTimePicker`, has that `name`, as a `Field.TextInput` does when it has no `name` of its own. To send the date and time as `2026-10-13T09:30` rather than the typed text, put `name` on the picker instead of the Root.

## Typed dates and times

`formatDateTime(value, { locale })` writes the date in numbers, as `formatDate` does, and the time in hours and minutes, or with seconds when it has seconds, such as `10/13/2026, 9:30 PM` in `en-US`, `13.10.2026, 21:30` in `de` and `2026. 10. 13. 오후 9:30` in `ko`. `parseDateTime(text, { locale })` reads typed text back into a `Temporal.PlainDateTime`, or returns `null` when the text is not one. It reads a date as [`parseDate`](/field/date-picker#typed-dates) does, then a time as [`parseTime`](/field/time-picker#typed-times) does, in the order the locale writes them, with or without the words the locale writes between them, such as `Oct 13, 2026 at 9:30 PM` or `10/13/2026 9:30 pm` in `en-US`. Where a number of the date meets a number of the time, as in `13.10.2026 21:30`, put a space or a comma between them. A date without a time is not read. Without `locale`, both use the runtime's own.

In the example, text that isn't a date and time clears the value, so the form sends none, and the field is marked invalid when it loses focus. `parseDateTime` doesn't check the picker's `min`, `max` or `isDateDisabled`, so check a typed value against them yourself.

## On its own

On its own, `Field.DateTimePicker` is the field's control. It shows the date in the locale's medium date style and the time in its short time style, or its medium style when the time has seconds, or `placeholder` while there is no value, and a press elsewhere in the field opens the popover. With a `name` on the Root, a hidden input sends the value the way HTML writes one, such as `2026-10-13T09:30`.

The popover opens and closes through `open` with `onOpenChange`, or `defaultOpen`. `onOpenChange` gets the reason it closed: `'select'` when a picked time makes the value, `'escape'`, `'outside'`, `'trigger'`, or `'disabled'` when the field becomes disabled. Open, the calendar takes focus on the date, or else today or the nearest date in range. Picking a date moves focus to the list of times, and picking a time before any date moves it to the calendar. Escape closes the popover, and focus goes back to where it was before it opened.

`min` and `max` limit both halves: the calendar offers only the dates between them, and on the first and last of those dates the list offers only the times in range. A date picked with a time out of range for it isn't reported until a time in range is picked. A date the calendar doesn't offer, such as one typed beside the picker, counts as no date: picking a time then moves focus to the calendar. `isDateDisabled` and `firstDayOfWeek` work as on [Field.DatePicker](/field/date-picker), and `step` as on [Field.TimePicker](/field/time-picker).

## Temporal

Todae ships no Temporal polyfill. The picker's values, `formatDateTime` and `parseDateTime` are `Temporal.PlainDateTime`, so where the browser has no Temporal, the app loads a polyfill (see [Native platform](/platform)). Todae's types need TypeScript 6 or later, which declares `Temporal`.

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** On its own, the button takes `id`, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby` and `aria-describedby` from `Field.Root`, and native `disabled` and `aria-invalid` from the Root or a surrounding [Fieldset](/fieldset). Its label names it, so the date and time it shows are added to its `aria-describedby`. Beside a Field part or a control joined with `useField`, other than a `Field.ColorPicker`, `Field.DatePicker`, `Field.TimePicker` or another `Field.DateTimePicker`, such as a text input, that control takes the `id` and `aria-describedby` in its place, and the button keeps `disabled` and `aria-invalid` and is named from the `dateTimePicker` string in [StringsProvider](/utilities/strings-provider) unless it has its own `aria-label`. The button gets `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` while the popover is open. The popover is a `role="dialog"` named from the `dateTimePicker` string. It holds a Calendar, with the keyboard described on its page, and an [OptionList](/optionlist) of times named from the `timePicker` string, where arrow keys, Home and End move and Enter or Space picks. The glyph is `calendar` from [GlyphsProvider](/utilities/glyphs-provider).

**You supply:** A name, through a [Label](/label) whose `htmlFor` is the Root `id` or `aria-label` / `aria-labelledby` on the Root. The same value for a text input and the picker beside it, written with `formatDateTime` and read with `parseDateTime`.

**Watch for:** A server render can't know another control sits beside the button, so until the page hydrates, the button and that control both carry the field's `id`, and both send a value under the field's `name`. With a `value`, the server writes the date and time in its own locale, so pass `locale` when the picker renders on a server.

## When to use

### Field.DateTimePicker

**When:** A date and a time of day in one value, such as when a meeting starts or a flight departs.

**When not:** A date alone → [Field.DatePicker](/field/date-picker). A time alone → [Field.TimePicker](/field/time-picker). An instant in a time zone → this picker, with the zone added in your own code.
