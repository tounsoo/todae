# Field.DatePicker

`Field.DatePicker` is a button inside a [Field](/field) that opens a [Calendar](/calendar) in a [Popover](/popover). Its `value` is a `Temporal.PlainDate` or `null`, and `onValueChange` gets the date picked in the calendar, which then closes. It takes the calendar's `min`, `max`, `isDateDisabled`, `firstDayOfWeek` and `locale`.

```tsx
const id = useId();
const [date, setDate] = useState<Temporal.PlainDate | null>(null);
const [text, setText] = useState('');
const [invalid, setInvalid] = useState(false);

<Field.Group>
  <Label.Root htmlFor={id}>Start date</Label.Root>
  <Field.Root id={id} invalid={invalid}>
    <Field.TextInput
      value={text}
      onChange={(event) => {
        setText(event.target.value);
        setDate(parseDate(event.target.value));
        setInvalid(false);
      }}
      onBlur={() => {
        if (date) setText(formatDate(date));
        else setInvalid(text.trim() !== '');
      }}
    />
    <Field.DatePicker
      value={date}
      onValueChange={(next) => {
        setDate(next);
        setText(formatDate(next));
        setInvalid(false);
      }}
      showDate={false}
    />
  </Field.Root>
</Field.Group>
```

Here a [Field.TextInput](/field/text-input) in the same field lets people type the date too. A picked date is written into the text with `formatDate`, and typed text that `parseDate` reads as a date moves the calendar to its month. Beside a `Field.TextInput`, that input is the field's control. It takes the field's `id`, `name` and description, and a press elsewhere in the field focuses it. With `showDate={false}`, the button shows only a calendar glyph. The button sends the date with a form under its own `name` if it has one, and otherwise under the field's `name`, unless a Field part or a control joined with [`useField`](/utilities/use-field), other than a `Field.ColorPicker`, has that `name`, as a `Field.TextInput` does when it has no `name` of its own. To send the date as `2026-10-13` rather than the typed text, put `name` on the picker instead of the Root.

## Typed dates

`formatDate(date, { locale })` writes a date in the locale's numbers, such as `10/13/2026` in `en-US`, `13.10.2026` in `de` and `2026. 10. 13.` in `ko`. `parseDate(text, { locale })` reads typed text back into a `Temporal.PlainDate`, or returns `null` when the text is not a date. It reads a date in numbers in the order `formatDate` writes them, or as `Intl.DateTimeFormat` writes it in the locale's `medium`, `long` or `full` date style, such as `Oct 13, 2026` or `Tuesday, October 13, 2026` in `en-US`. Letter case doesn't matter, digits may be in any script, and the parts may be separated by any punctuation, symbols or spaces. The month may be any of its long or short names, such as `Oct` or `October`. A weekday may be long, short or left out, but must be the date's. The style's other words, such as `de` in `13 de octubre de 2026`, may be replaced by a space. Three numbers that start with a year of four or more digits, such as `2026-10-13`, are read as year, month and day, unless the locale writes the year first in another order. A year is a Gregorian year, whatever the locale's own calendar. A year of one or two digits is read as the nearest such year to the current one, so a date about 50 years away needs all four digits, and a year of three digits is not read. Without `locale`, both use the runtime's own.

In the example, text that isn't a date clears the date, so the form sends no date, and the field is marked invalid when it loses focus. `parseDate` doesn't check the picker's `min`, `max` or `isDateDisabled`, so check a typed date against them yourself.

## On its own

On its own, `Field.DatePicker` is the field's control. It shows the date in `locale`'s medium style, or `placeholder` while there is none, and a press elsewhere in the field opens the calendar. With a `name` on the Root, a hidden input sends the date the way HTML writes one, such as `2026-10-13`.

The calendar opens and closes through `open` with `onOpenChange`, or `defaultOpen`. `onOpenChange` gets the reason it closed: `'select'` when a date is picked, `'escape'`, `'outside'`, `'trigger'`, or `'disabled'` when the field becomes disabled. Open, the calendar puts focus on the date, else on today or the nearest date in range. Escape closes it, and focus goes back to where it was before it opened.

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** On its own, the button takes `id`, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby` and `aria-describedby` from `Field.Root`, and native `disabled` and `aria-invalid` from the Root or a surrounding [Fieldset](/fieldset). Its label names it, so the date it shows is added to its `aria-describedby`. Beside a Field part or a control joined with `useField`, other than a `Field.ColorPicker` or another `Field.DatePicker`, such as a text input, that control takes the `id` and `aria-describedby` in its place, and the button keeps `disabled` and `aria-invalid` and is named from the `datePicker` string in [StringsProvider](/utilities/strings-provider) unless it has its own `aria-label`. The button gets `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` while the calendar is open. The popover is a `role="dialog"` named from the `datePicker` string, and the calendar inside is keyed as on [Calendar](/calendar). The glyph is `calendar` from [GlyphsProvider](/utilities/glyphs-provider).

**You supply:** A name, through a [Label](/label) whose `htmlFor` is the Root `id` or `aria-label` / `aria-labelledby` on the Root. The same date for a text input and the picker beside it, written with `formatDate` and read with `parseDate`.

**Watch for:** A server render can't know another control sits beside the button, so until the page hydrates, the button and that control both carry the field's `id`, and both send a value under the field's `name`. With a `value`, the server writes that date in its own locale, so pass `locale` when the picker renders on a server.

## When to use

### Field.DatePicker

**When:** A date in a form where people benefit from seeing a month, such as a booking or a deadline.

**When not:** A date people know by heart, such as a birthday → a [Field.TextInput](/field/text-input) read with `parseDate`. A calendar shown on the page → [Calendar](/calendar).
