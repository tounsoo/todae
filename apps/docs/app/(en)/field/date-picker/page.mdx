# Field.DatePicker

`Field.DatePicker` is a button inside a [Field](/field) that opens a [Calendar](/calendar) in a [Popover](/popover). Its `value` is a `Temporal.PlainDate` or `null`, and `onValueChange` gets the date picked in the calendar, which then closes. It takes the calendar's `min`, `max`, `isDateDisabled`, `firstDayOfWeek` and `locale`.

```tsx
const id = useId();
const [date, setDate] = useState<Temporal.PlainDate | null>(null);

<Field.Group>
  <Label.Root htmlFor={id}>Start date</Label.Root>
  <Field.Root id={id}>
    <Field.DateInput value={date} onValueChange={setDate} />
    <Field.DatePicker value={date} onValueChange={setDate} />
  </Field.Root>
</Field.Group>
```

</Example>

Here a [Field.DateInput](/field/date-input) in the same field lets people type the date too. The two share one state, so a picked date fills the input, and a typed date moves the calendar to its month. Beside a `Field.DateInput`, that input is the field's control. It takes the field's `id`, `name` and description, and a press elsewhere in the field focuses it. The button shows only a calendar glyph, and in Chromium browsers the input's own picker button is hidden.

## On its own

On its own, `Field.DatePicker` is the field's control. It shows the date in `locale`'s medium style, or `placeholder` while there is none, and a press elsewhere in the field opens the calendar. With a `name` on the Root, a hidden input sends the date as an ISO string, such as `2026-10-13`.

<Example block>

</Example>

The calendar opens and closes through `open` with `onOpenChange`, or `defaultOpen`. `onOpenChange` gets the reason it closed: `'select'` when a date is picked, `'escape'`, `'outside'`, `'trigger'`, or `'disabled'` when the field becomes disabled. Open, the calendar puts focus on the date, else on today or the nearest date in range. Escape closes it, and focus goes back to where it was before it opened.

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** On its own, the button takes `id`, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby` and `aria-describedby` from `Field.Root`, and native `disabled` and `aria-invalid` from the Root or a surrounding [Fieldset](/fieldset). Its label names it, so the date it shows is added to its `aria-describedby`. Beside another control in the field, such as a date input, that control takes the `id` and `aria-describedby` in its place, and the button keeps `disabled` and `aria-invalid` and is named from the `datePicker` string in [StringsProvider](/utilities/strings-provider) unless it has its own `aria-label`. The button gets `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` while the calendar is open. The popover is a `role="dialog"` named from the `datePicker` string, and the calendar inside is keyed as on [Calendar](/calendar). The glyph is `calendar` from [GlyphsProvider](/utilities/glyphs-provider).

**You supply:** A name, through a [Label](/label) whose `htmlFor` is the Root `id` or `aria-label` / `aria-labelledby` on the Root. The same state for a `Field.DateInput` and the picker beside it.

**Watch for:** A server render can't know another control sits beside the button, so until the page hydrates, the button and that control both carry the field's `id`.

## When to use

### Field.DatePicker

**When:** A date in a form where people benefit from seeing a month, such as a booking or a deadline.

**When not:** A date people know by heart, such as a birthday → [Field.DateInput](/field/date-input) alone. A calendar shown on the page → [Calendar](/calendar). A date with a time → [Field.DateTimeInput](/field/date-time-input).
