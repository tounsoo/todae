# Steps

`Steps.Root` shows a sequence of steps and where each one is, such as an agent's plan while it works or a checklist. Each `Steps.Item` takes a `status`: `done`, `current`, `pending` or `error`. The steps and their order are your data; Todae draws a marker for each status and the line that joins them.

```tsx
<Steps.Root aria-label="Plan">
  {plan.map((step) => (
    <Steps.Item key={step.id} status={step.status}>
      {step.title}
    </Steps.Item>
  ))}
</Steps.Root>
```

## Parts

- `Steps.Root` is an `ol` with `role="list"`, so Safari keeps it a list without its markers.
- `Steps.Item` is an `li` with a marker, then its content. Its `status` sets `data-status` and the marker: a `done` step shows the `check` glyph on a filled circle and has its text struck through, the `current` step shows a [Loader](/loader) spinner and takes `aria-current="step"`, a `pending` step shows an empty circle, and a step with an `error` shows the `error` glyph in the critical colors. `pending` is the default.

The markers and their colors come from the `steps.marker.*` tokens, the text colors from `steps.fg.*`, the line from `steps.line.*`, and the space between steps from `steps.gap`.

## Status words

The marker is hidden from screen readers. Each step starts with a visually hidden status word instead, so a screen reader reads "Done: Read the failing test". The words come from the `stepDone`, `stepCurrent`, `stepPending` and `stepError` strings of [StringsProvider](/utilities/strings-provider), "Done", "In progress", "Not started" and "Failed" in English. Pass `statusLabel` to an item for a word of your own, such as "Skipped" on a `done` step.

## Accessible composition

**Todae handles:** An ordered list, a status word read before each step, `aria-current="step"` on the running step, and markers kept out of the accessible name.

**You supply:** A name for the list with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` when the page has more than one. An announcement when a step finishes or fails, if the person should hear it without moving to the list, such as through [useAnnouncer](/utilities/use-announcer). A status word that fits when the default doesn't.

**Watch for:** Showing a failure by color alone: say what went wrong in the step's text too. Putting a long step's details in the item, where the line-through of a done step crosses them all.

## When to use

**When:** A plan or checklist whose steps run in order, each with a status.

**When not:** One running task → [Loader](/loader) or [Progress](/progress). A trail of pages → [Breadcrumbs](/breadcrumbs).
